Changelog

Version history for Yet Another Idle RPG. Pick a version to unfold it.

Current version: v0.6.7
  • The guild's board is real. Once the clerk has told you about it, a new Guild work tab in your journal shows what is posted: three jobs, at your rank and one either side. Work above your rank pays better and is harder to survive.
  • Every job is either something to kill or something to bring, and how much of it depends on whether the notice is plain, long or brutal work. The board is repainted once per in-game day.
  • You can take one job at a time. What you take comes off the board, stays taken when the day turns, and is still yours after you reload.
  • The clerk is not taking finished work in yet — that is the next piece. The panel says so where you take a job, so nothing you do is wasted: what you have taken is written down and will keep.
  • The traces at the water lead somewhere. If you have read both of them, there is now something to be found in the places you read them — not an errand and nothing to click on: it happens, or it does not, while you are stood there doing something else.
  • It is very rare, and that is the point of it. Almost nobody will see it, and it is out there either way. Once it has happened, the lake reads differently.
  • Your standing with the guild is a rank now, from F up to SSS, and the Data panel says which one you hold and how much more you need for the next.
  • It is read from the standing you already have, so nothing was reset: everything the guild has ever paid you counts, and doing all of its existing work puts you at D.
  • Books can ask for a skill now, and the first one that does is The Long Guard — the second half of a swordsman's education, which wants you at Sword mastery before it will tell you anything. The back room keeps a copy.
  • Every other book still reads freely. Opening one you are not ready for does not hide it or refuse silently: it tells you which skill it wants and at what level.
  • Once you have looked into the rock shelters, the Forest lake reads differently. On the far side a run of reeds lies pushed flat in a line about the width of a cart — brown where they lie and green where they stand, and like that since before the first time you came.
  • Nothing new has happened at the lake. You are just the sort of person who sees it now.
  • Once you have read the shallows at the lake, the rock shelters under the waterfall are worth looking into. One of them has been used: the stone worn smooth in a shape longer than you are, and a floor of crab shells opened rather than crushed.
  • Before that, the shelters are just holes with crabs in them and the game says so — the option is there and it tells you why it is not worth your time yet.
  • Afterwards the basin has a second sound. It had exactly one.
  • Something comes down to the Forest lake to drink. You can read the shallows and find out a little about it — four toes, sand pushed up hard behind them, and too big for a heron.
  • How much you get out of the sand depends on your Perception, and a poorer eye is told what a better one would have read rather than shown nothing.
  • Afterwards the lake has three sounds it did not have before, mixed in with the frogs and the ducks. Nothing is named and nothing is answered.
  • A Only what I can make box under the crafting pages' buttons. Tick it and every recipe you are short of something for is hidden, on all three pages.
  • The equipment page can answer that question for the first time: it now knows whether you are holding a component for each slot a recipe needs, and how many complete sets you could assemble.
  • A recipe you have never actually made is marked with a small ring beside its name, on all three crafting pages. It goes out the first time you make one.
  • The mark is kept in your save, so it survives a reload. A save from before this update has made nothing as far as it can say, and each ring clears as you craft.
  • A bed you have just unlocked appears in the fast travel list straight away. It was being registered a moment after the list had already been rebuilt, so it stayed missing until something else happened to redraw it.
  • There is a stall on the town square. Cry the news for the square once and the stallholder is someone you have met.
  • It is the town's only trade in anything but a cooked meal — both shops in the region were cafes selling the same ten dishes, so anything else meant walking back to the village or down into the slums.
  • What it sells is what a market sells and what nobody else did: wool and flax and the cloth they make, charcoal, brick, tallow, sinew, sap and sawn planks. None of it was on any shelf in the game before.
  • And a fishing pole. Pickaxes, axes, sickles and shovels were all buyable somewhere; the pole was the one tool you could not buy, for an activity three regions offer. The cheap two only — the ash and hickory poles are still something you make.
  • The town square has something to do in it besides its three errands. It had no activities at all — ten at the village against none here.
  • Run the square once you have chased the pigeons for it, and stand the night watch once the square trusts you to judge between two bakers. Each is opened by the errand on the standing ladder that earns it.
  • The watch pays, and pays what the village patrol pays: it is the same work. It is night work, though — between 20:00 and 06:00, which is what makes it an alternative to the village's day patrol rather than a copy of it.
  • A Try again button sits beside Finish when an attempt can simply be run again. A failed lock keeps its chest, so you can go straight back at it instead of leaving the screen and clicking in from the location list.
  • It only appears where it would actually work. Pick the lock successfully and the chest is spent, so there is nothing to try again on and no button. It is not an undo either: it starts the attempt exactly as clicking in from outside would, with the same requirements and the same cost.
  • The last two quests of the bay's story reach your journal now. Out on the Ebb and One Unweighed Crate were written, and every piece of work that was supposed to advance them was written too, but nothing ever handed them to you — so the whole of that work counted for nothing and the journal never mentioned it. The tallyman gives you each of them at the point in the conversation where he was always going to.
  • The tallyman also gets his line about the guild back. He tells you he will not make it a guild matter, and in saying so tells you that you can — and that half of what he says had been silently dropped.
  • The bay opens. The harbour tallyman had been left with nothing to say: the line that opens the rest of his conversation was promised by his greeting, and anyone who heard that greeting before the promise could be kept was sealed out of the whole region — no talk, no work, no way on to the salt house or the flats.
  • Anything else a finished piece of work had promised and never handed over is handed over on loading, in the same pass. Conversations, jobs, traders, beds, titles. You are told how many.
  • It cannot give you the same thing twice, and it never hands out money, items or experience — only the doors that were supposed to open.
  • An exported save now names the version that wrote it: yet-another-idle-rpg 2026-09-01 18_06_03 v0.7.28.txt. The version goes after the date so a folder of exports still sorts chronologically by name.
  • Standing you earned before it counted for anything is paid to you when your save loads. The swampland deliveries began granting reputation in v0.7.21, and anyone who had already made them could never receive it — leaving the tribe at nought with nothing left in the game able to raise it, and the chief's confidence permanently out of reach.
  • You are told in the message log when this happens, and how much. It cannot pay twice: it compares what your finished work owes against what you have, so a second load finds nothing to do and a player who earned it normally is untouched.
  • A fourth way to sort the bag and the storage chest: Newest, which puts what you picked up last at the top. It ignores the category rules the other sorts obey, because a sword just found should not be sitting below every scrap of leather you are carrying.
  • Adding to a stack you already have counts as getting it, so the stack moves back up. The order survives a save, and an old save keeps the order it was written in — which is the order things were first picked up anyway.
  • The sorting buttons say Name, Value, Type, Newest now instead of spelling out "Sort by name" on each one, and the full wording moved to the tooltip. Skills read Name, Level, Progress the same way.
  • Traders keep three buttons. Their stock is put out when you walk in, so "newest" would only be telling you the order it was generated in.
  • The bay's two seasonal jobs are on the board all year now. Out of season they refuse you and say why — nothing is being landed, because the Marrowmoth works the ebb in spring and in autumn and the quay is a row of sheds the rest of the time. Before, they simply were not there, and a winter visitor had no way to know the work existed.
  • Durations spoken out in full are translated now. The reading and training panels used to say "2 days 15 hours 22 minutes" whatever language you played in — in English that read fine, in Turkish it was the only untranslated thing on the screen. It says "2 gün 15 saat 21 dakika" there now.
  • The short form (2D15h22m) is unchanged. A letter is not a word.
  • The Discoveries and Lore panels update while you are looking at them. They were rebuilt when you opened the tab and when you touched a filter, and not in between — so picking something up with the panel on screen left the count where it was until you switched tabs and came back.
  • Nothing is rebuilt when the panel is closed, so this costs nothing while you are not reading it.
  • Seven pieces of clothing were showing you their internal name instead of a real one. In English that looked fine, because the internal name is English; in Turkish it was the one untranslated thing on the screen. The snakeskin boots and leggings, the linen leggings and bandanna, the wool shirt, and both chainmail vests now read properly in both languages.
  • The swampland tribe has standing now, and the five deliveries you were already doing raise it — the cook's order, the tailor's flax, both of the tanner's runs and walking the line with the scout. 300 in total. If you have done them, the row is on your sheet the next time you look.
  • The scout's errand used to give you nothing at all for doing it. It gives standing and experience now, which is what the other four always did.
  • At 200 the chief has something to say about it, and it is not a reward: he explains that the tribe does not say a thing like that out loud, and what it does instead is stop watching where you put your feet.
  • The chest no longer keeps handing you the same wool scarf. What was two items at high chances is now eight, and seven of them are what somebody actually buries: bundles of hides, fangs, tusks and bone. A second bundle is not clutter — those are the things a butchering upgrade wants fifty of.
  • Which also means the box is fuller. It used to give coin and nothing else on two opens in five; now that is one in five, and about half of what comes out on top of the coin is a stack rather than a single thing. The scarf is still in there, at a twelfth of its old chance, because it is the personal thing in the box and it should be the rare one.
  • A chest no longer holds the same thing every time. Coin and the practice are certain; a scarf, a dagger, a false bottom with more coin under it are each rolled on their own, so two chests are rarely the same haul.
  • And sometimes there is a needle under the lid, set there on purpose and not a clean one. It costs you health for a while and never the chest — a cost, not a punishment. Somebody who knows locks notices it more often: the chance runs from three in ten at Lockpicking 0 down to six in a hundred at 30.
  • Underneath both: a reward can carry a chance now, and a reward can be something that hurts. Neither was possible before — an action had one success path and every reward on it was certain.
  • Hunting turns up a locked chest now, rarely — four in a thousand kills, from the bear, the warthog, the mountain goat and the alligator. You did not take it off the animal. It was under the same overhang, and the lock is not complicated; it is just not yours.
  • So there is a Lockpicking skill, and a place in the village to sit down with a chest and a bent length of wire. At level 0 you get it open one time in five; at 30, nine times in ten. Failing costs the attempt and nothing else — the chest stays shut rather than gone, and the time already spent stays spent on it. That is also how the skill is learned, since a fifth of attempts succeed from the very start.
  • You can also just sell the thing unopened, for a fraction of what is usually inside. Whether the certain small amount beats the uncertain larger one is your call.
  • On a new moon, the second box in the slums has a box under it. What is in that one is not rarer — it is the same four hide capes the row already sells, at a quality no shop in the game is allowed to offer. Every shelf in the game stops at 120%. This one starts at 130%.
  • It is there for about a week in every month and then it is not. The old woman will tell you which kind of night it is, and she has a view on where a cloak that good comes from, and on asking.
  • Conditions can now name a phase of the moon, the way they already name a season. A season is a quarter of the year, which is right for a boat that comes twice a year and far too slow for something meant to turn up occasionally.
  • The man in the corner of the slums has a second box, and at Slums standing 300 he takes the lid off in front of you. What is in it is not new: it is the handful of things nobody sells and almost nobody makes — a pristine goat horn is fifty goat horns, and a goat horn is a one-in-fifty drop. He does not always have one. He is not holding out on you when he does not.
  • The old woman will tell you about it, on the same standing that puts you on her roster. She has opinions about where any of it came from, and a piece of advice about asking.
  • The Discoveries panel lists those five as things the slums sells before you can buy them there, which is the point — a shelf you cannot see is not something to work towards.
  • The broker in the town square answers a stranger and a regular differently. His old answer — "Bring me a quantity and I will give you a number. Bring me a story and I will give you nothing" — was the only one he had, and it is a stranger's answer. He keeps every word of it right up until the town knows who you are, and then he stops reciting the rule and tells you something instead. Nothing you do makes it happen except being known.
  • Which needed something the game could not previously say. Standing was only ever a floor to climb over, so a place could get warmer and never be cold; a line could be written for a regular and not for a stranger. It can be written for either now, and the first thing that used it is a man who stops pretending not to know you.
  • The antique collector will sell you one more thing, and it is the last. He wrote it himself: forty years of notes on crabs, on turtles and on the alligator — where a shell parts, which claw is worth taking off whole, what the hide is under all that mud. Nobody has ever asked him for it. It is the longest read in the game and the dearest thing anyone sells, and it teaches a skill you cannot learn any other way.
  • Shellwork, that skill, multiplies what water creatures drop, the way Butchering does for beasts. It stacks with Butchering on the six creatures that are both, and it does nothing anywhere else — a crab claw or a turtle shell at half a percent is exactly the kind of drop that needs help.
  • The help page now explains drop rates at all, including the one thing nothing told you: fighting several enemies at once lowers what each of them drops.
  • Fixes a recipe that the previous version broke. Giving fish dishes a quality also gave the fish fillet one, and the fish steak recipe asks for a fillet by name — and asking for something by name only ever looked for the plain, quality-less version of it. So a fillet cut from a good catfish was invisible to the recipe that wants it: ten in the bag and the steak read as unavailable. Anything a recipe names is now found whatever quality it carries, and the poorest of a stack is spent first so the good ones keep.
  • A recipe tooltip now tells you the quality it is going to make, when the materials you have carry one. It reads the same numbers the craft itself will, so what it promises is what you get.
  • A fish keeps its quality when you cook it. A recipe that makes an item now rolls the result's quality around the quality of what went in, so a good trout makes a good fried fish and a poor one makes a poor meal — and the dish shows the number, coloured, the way a sword does. Until today the fish's quality simply stopped existing at the pan: every dish came out unmarked, and unmarked is priced as though it were exactly 100%. Fishing is the only thing outside the workshop that gives an item a quality, so the fish are what this is about; a recipe fed materials with no quality of their own behaves exactly as it always has.
  • Which also means a dish is worth what its fish was worth. Early on, when your fishing and your cooking are both poor, that is less than the flat price a meal used to fetch. A few levels in it is more, and it keeps climbing to twice as much. It is on the item either way, so you can see which it is.
  • The four fish dishes — the skewer, the fried fish, the fillet and the steak — show a quality now, and the fillet passes its own along to the steak.
  • The tallyman will not put the Marrowmoth on the guild's books. He explains at length why not, and in explaining he tells you that you could. The guild clerk will do it — she opens a file, writes the hull, the two springs, and stops at the fourth column exactly as he did — and it will be known that it came from the docks. Nothing makes you ask. The arc finishes either way. But the row hears about it, and the old woman of the slums has something to say that is not an accusation and is not nothing.
  • Standing can no longer go below zero. Nothing subtracted from it before today, so nothing had ever noticed that the character sheet only draws a region once it is above zero — a player in the negative would have seen no row at all, with no way to tell they were in a hole or how deep.
  • The boatman out to the Marrowmoth costs 6,000 instead of 25,000. He is a per-trip service and he says so — "I will not wait" — but at the old price you could afford him about once in the whole game, which quietly turned the dearer way across the mud into a way you took once and then never again. It is one job's pay for one boat ride now. Wading it is still free and still turns you back when the water does, and the firm line is still there if the row knows you.
  • Both cafés off the town square sell something now. They never have: the Cat café was a room with nine background noises and nothing in it, and the nekomimi proprietress offered you a menu she could not actually serve. Walk into the Cat café and the counter is open; ask the proprietress about her offer and hers is. Ten things between them — fresh bread, bread kwas, cooked clam, crab bisque, kingsized frog legs, fish steak and more — and the town has a market of its own for the first time, which means what you sell in it moves prices in the slums and nowhere else.
  • The salt house sells a book, which is the first the bay has ever had: Nothing Bites Here, in which somebody who spent a working life on this coast wrote down, in order, every place along it where you will catch nothing at all. Read it and you can fish off the quay — the harbour had no work of its own until now — for sea fish, and for clams, which had exactly one other source in the game.
  • Fixed: Discoveries had stopped knowing where white iron ore and black iron ore come from. When the salt house's shelf started changing with the season two versions ago, the panel lost every item that trader sells — and the bay is the only place in the game that sells either ore. Nothing looked broken; the page simply had no answer.
  • Action tooltips no longer run off the bottom of the window. On the last row of a long action list a tall tooltip was simply drawn below the cursor and left there, half of it past the edge of the screen. It now opens upwards when there is no room below, rather than being slid up to sit on top of the row you are pointing at — which would keep it on screen and put the thing you are reading about behind the thing you are reading.
  • How you are standing changes what things do to you, not just what your numbers are. Put your point through the middle of an ant swarm and the rest of it arrives anyway, up your sleeves and into every gap you have — unless you are sweeping, in which case they go backwards instead. A frog's toxins do not care how hard you hit; they care how much of you is in front of them, so Berserker's Stride catches twice as much of it and Defensive Measures half. And a dragonfly's stinger finds a body that has committed to a swing far more easily than one that has not stopped moving. Four enemies react to your stance now, and none of them gained a stat line to do it.
  • Tier 5 can be made. Where the flats scour deepest there is a seam of something almost black and far heavier than sand has any right to be — a smith's lodestone drags through it and comes out furred — and it is only above water in the two seasons the Marrowmoth is on the mud, so nobody sells it because nobody can get at it. Dig it, smelt it with white or black iron ore the way Atratan ore makes steel out of iron, and white steel and black steel come out. Thirty-six components have existed, finished, since before this fork and nothing in the game could produce a single one of them: every weapon head, every handle, both shield bases, and chainmail and plate for all five armour slots. All thirty-six are craftable now. The tier that could only ever be shopped for has something you have to go and get.
  • The crate opens, and a crate is not a lock — the difficulty is putting it back, because the man who lashed it will be the next person to look at it. Fail the reading and you work out, before you cut anything, that you could not remake it, so you sit with your back against it until the water tells you to go. Get it right and there is straw, a bed cut out of something grey that is not felt and is not cork and does not compress, and one closed band the size of a wrist, in a metal that is not iron or steel or bronze and takes no mark from a nail, cut through all the way round with squares that turn and come back to their own beginning. You put it all back exactly as it was. Then you take the description across the square to the man who held the other one for about the time it takes to boil water, and he puts his magnifying glass in the drawer before he answers.
  • She is lying on the mud a half-mile off the hard, and there are three ways to reach her. Walk it, if you are steady enough — free, and the water comes back on its own schedule rather than yours, so you may well end up back on the quay black to the knee. Pay a boatman, if you can stand the price he names for a hull nobody has posted work for. Or get walked out on the firm line by somebody who works the mud, if the row knows you well enough — in his prints, not beside them. All three end at the same ladder, so nothing out here is behind a skill you do not have. Below the cargo deck the ship is heeled over and everything was lashed by people who assumed it would be level; go the length of it in the dark and right aft, on its own, with four feet of clearance nothing else on that deck was given, there is one crate. You do not touch it. The water is coming back and you can hear it through the hull.
  • Three ways to find out something the manifest does not say, and they do not tell you the same thing. The guild will let you go through the seal book if it thinks enough of you: the mark on that line is a house seal, cut by a good hand, from a house that is not in the book and never was. The men who carried it will talk to somebody who knows how to ask: two of them took it off, it was light, and it went south on a cart with its wheels wrapped in sacking. The factor keeps copies of everything that comes inland, and if the town has decided about you he will let you at the shelf: the same empty line two springs back, with one letter started in the destination column and stopped. Any one of the three finishes the quest — the other two are more of the picture, not a second copy of it. Guild standing is a thing now, and this is where you start earning it.
  • There is work on the quay while she is in, and nobody is paying for it. Lend a hand getting her cargo off — a day on the plank, no name taken, no money — and afterwards nobody minds you reading the tally nailed inside the shed door. Six columns, five complete lines, and one that is not: four of its six were written empty, and the ruling underneath them is unbroken, so nothing was rubbed out. The tallyman will tell you why there is no weight in the weight column, and then he will tell you it is the second time. He will not tell you it is the same crate, because he does not know. He knows he wrote both lines. The journal has a new page for what you are piecing together, separate from who told you what.
  • Something comes into the bay twice a year, and nothing tells you so. In spring and in autumn the salt house is holding what a ship has just landed rather than what is left of the last one — the metal from very far away is on the floor instead of one sack at the back of it — the quay has a good deal more going on than it does the rest of the year, and the guild clerk has an opinion about a forty-ton hull that ties up and posts no work at all. No notification, no quest and no marker: you are either standing there in the right season or you are not.
  • The journal keeps its contents inside itself again. Titles, Discoveries and the quest list had all been spilling over the bottom edge and printing on top of the skills box underneath, and the quest list was sliding under its own "hide completed" bar. One cause for all of it: the row of tabs along the top wraps onto another line, and everything below it had been given a height that assumed it did not. Adding the Titles tab was what tipped it over. The panels now take whatever the tabs leave, however many tabs there turn out to be, and the quest list scrolls with the hide-completed bar staying put underneath it.
  • The original game has its own link in the corner now, next to this fork's. Two GitHub marks side by side would say nothing about which repository each one opens, so the original's is dimmed until you point at it, and all three icons down there finally say what they are in your own language - the tooltips had been English no matter what you set the game to.
  • The wet woods have an ending. Clear the drowned grove all the way through and the flax comes up in long uncut stands, because nothing goes through there any more to trample it - you can go back and cut it, once, for more than you can use in a season. Nobody is there to see it and nobody turns up: that is the point of the place. The region had been stopping rather than closing, with nothing to mark having finished with it.
  • The journal has a Titles tab: a record of what you have done, which is the other half of the Lore page's record of what you were told. Twelve to earn, from things the game was already counting - a hundred wolf rats, a thousand kills of anything, five hundred damage in one blow, a hundred things made and not ruined, and being known in the village, the slums and the town at once. They carry no bonus and are not meant to: a title is a record, not a reward, and the ones you have not earned are not listed, because that would make it a list of chores instead.
  • Plate armour can be made. The game has always known what steel, white iron and black iron plate armour looks like - twenty-five pieces of it across five slots - and there was nothing to forge any of it from, because no metal plate existed as a material. Not a missing tier: the whole line had no first rung, steel included, while the shell plate a turtle drops worked fine. Three plates now forge from three ingots each, one band harder than the chainmail of the same metal, and every helmet, chest, leg, glove and boot piece in those three metals is reachable. Fifteen pieces. White and black steel stay out until there is a forge hot enough for them.
  • Two people notice what you have built. The broker under the green awning has a line once the town knows your name, and he does with a reputation what he does with everything else, which is put a price on it; the old woman of the slums has one once the row puts you on its roster, and she tells you what that costs rather than what it is worth. Neither resolves anything. Both open on standing alone - no quest, no unlock - and both go into the lore panel.
  • The town square has something to do in it. It is the town's centre of life and it was a room you walked through: a fountain, pigeons, a newspaper crier and two bakers calling each other stale, all of it noise and none of it anything you could join. Three things open there as the town gets to know you - keeping the pigeons off the bread, taking the crier's shift, and being handed the casting vote in the bread argument, which is what standing actually buys in a square. All three are built out of what was already in its background noises rather than added next to them.
  • The slums pay in standing now. Three things to do there open as your reputation rises: minding the scales at the factor's shed, taking your turn on the row's night watch, and standing surety for someone at the town gate - which is the first thing in the game that turns standing in the slums into standing in the town. Until now 350 slums reputation was earnable and nothing at all read it back except a trader's margin, so the number rose and meant nothing.
  • Action buttons stopped printing paragraphs. Six actions carry a narrative sentence in the field the model documents as "text on the button", and the button was drawing it verbatim: "You mark it out behind the well. Within the hour there are more people carrying brick than you asked for." was the label on Build the village hearth. Each of them already had the short label written and unused. A check now holds every action label to 80 characters, in both languages.
  • The dev console gained add_best_effect(), the counterpart to give_best(): every good effect at once for a duration you choose, instead of naming Coffee, Spark of Inspiration and Well hydrated one at a time. Which effects count as good is read off the templates rather than listed in the console, so one added tomorrow is included the moment it is tagged - and a check now fails the build if anything tagged as a buff has a wholly harmful stat profile, since the command trusts that tag. 22 effects today.
  • Loading a save stopped part-way through, so quests came back empty and favourited places vanished from fast travel. Splitting saving and loading out into their own file last version left one name behind: the loader reached for the effect templates without importing them. An unresolved name is a legitimate reference to a runtime global as far as the bundler is concerned, so it built cleanly and threw only once somebody loaded a save - and everything in the loader after that line never ran. The check that exists to catch exactly this now reads names used as values and through a spread, not only names being called.
  • The game had also stopped remembering which bed to return to and where you last fought. The same split renamed two fields, and the rename reached inside the quoted save keys as well - so the save wrote them under names the loader did not read. The value came back empty, the next save wrote nothing at all, and the keys left the file. Both are written under their proper names again, and a new check requires every key the save writes to be a key the load reads, because this one broke nothing loudly enough to notice.
  • If you played v0.6.54, that version wrote its losses into your save: favourited places, kill counts and the two locations above were saved as empty. Progress made in the meantime is untouched - it is only those fields that were cleared. An export taken before v0.6.54 still holds them.
  • Discoveries stopped running off the bottom of the journal. Its filters wrap onto a second row once there are enough of them, and the list underneath had a fixed height that knew nothing about that - so the box grew past its own edge. The list now takes whatever the controls leave, at any number of rows. The same was true of Lore and is fixed with it.
