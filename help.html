Questions

The less mystery there is, the less fun it might be. Or not - that is entirely up to you.

The last combat areas are:
  • On the mountain: 'Gentle mountain slope'
  • In the caves: 'Writhing tunnel'
  • In the forest: 'Bear den' and 'Red ant nest'
  • Down the river: 'The swamplands'
Past the swamp the story keeps going without combat. The plains lead to the coast road and the bay, and the bay is where the written content currently stops: a harbour, a salt house, and — twice a year, while a certain ship is lying on the mud — the tidal flats and what is below her cargo deck.

Not yet. Some of its vocabulary is already in the game and you may have noticed it: there are Wands and Staffs skills, your character sheet has a mana pool, and intuition is the stat magic reads. None of it is wired to anything, and there is no wand or staff to be had, so those two skills cannot currently be raised at all.

It is planned as its own arc rather than as a feature dropped into an existing one: mana spent during ordinary combat, spells with damage and with buff and debuff effects, magic power as a real stat that gear and skills raise. That is a larger change than any single region has been, so it comes after the current story rather than beside it.
  • Actions are the one-off jobs a place offers: reading a set of tracks, picking a lock, standing between the pigeons and the bread. Each takes a stretch of time and most can fail — either because you were never able to begin (it says which skill it wanted) or because the attempt itself went wrong.
    A failed attempt leaves a Try again button under it, so you can repeat it where you stand instead of walking out of the location and back in. Actions that keep their progress keep it across the retry.
  • Activities are the opposite: passive, repeatable, and stopped when you choose. Some gather things, some only train a skill, and some pay. The town square has two of the plain kind — running, which is nothing but exercise, and patrolling, which is a watch somebody else would otherwise have to stand. Its stalls also keep a trader, and there is paid one-off work there: keeping the pigeons off the bread, crying the news for five hours, and settling an argument between two bakers that is older than either of them.
  • Reading is its own thing again: a book takes time, raises Literacy, and leaves a permanent bonus when it is finished. Most books ask nothing of you. A few now require a skill — The Long Guard is the second half of a swordsman's education and wants Swords at level 20 — and opening one you are not ready for does not hide it or refuse in silence: it names the skill it wants and the level.

The journal is the panel on the right with the row of buttons across the top. Everything in it is a record of what you have already done - none of it is a thing to do.

  • Quests — what you have been asked to do and what you have finished. Long quests list their tasks; a task that is hidden until you reach it says so rather than being missing. Hide completed clears out the ones you are done with.
  • Bestiary — one entry per creature, written the first time you kill one. It carries the creature's stats, everywhere it can be found, and how many of them you have killed. If something is not in here you have never fought it.
  • Anthology — the books you have read, and what each one gave you. A book you are part way through says how much is left.
  • Discoveries — the answer to where does this come from. Every item you have ever held, with each place it can be gathered, the creature that drops it, the trader who sells it, or the recipe that makes it. Each source has a Go button that walks you there if the place is open to you. There is a search box, and two filters: hide the things you can only buy, and hide the things with no known source. It also keeps a where to train list, which is the same question asked about skills instead of items.
  • Lore — what people have told you. Most dialogue can only be read once, so anything worth keeping is copied here: where you left off, the threads you are piecing together across several conversations, and who said what. It is searchable, which matters most for the threads that run through more than one town.
  • Titles — what you have done. Most are earned from your own totals as you pass them, and a few are given by the story. They fill in on their own; nothing has to be claimed.
  • Guild work and Data have sections of their own on this page.

The clerk at the Adventurer's guild in town keeps a board of paid work. She will not mention it until you have talked to her, and until she does the Guild work tab in your journal only tells you where to go.

Your standing with the guild is also a rank, from F at the bottom up through E, D, C, B, A, S and SS to SSS. It is read from Guild standing rather than awarded separately, so everything the guild has ever paid you already counts towards it. The Data tab shows the rank you hold and how much more standing the next one needs.

The board shows work at your rank and one either side of it. Taking something above your rank promotes you faster and asks for a great deal more of it; taking something below is easier and pays less. Every notice is either something to kill or something to bring, and how much depends on whether it is plain, long or brutal work.

The board is repainted once per in-game day. You can hold up to three jobs at once: what you take comes off the board, stays yours when the day turns, and is still yours after you reload.
Some notices carry a deadline and most do not — the panel shows the days left on the ones that do. Miss it and the guild takes the same standing giving up would have cost, without asking: the clerk simply writes the date beside your name. It is said in the message log rather than the panel, because a job can run out while you are nowhere near the journal. A hunt counts kills of anything carrying the tag it names, and only from the moment you took it. A fetch counts what you are holding, across every stack of it, so quality does not divide the count.

Take the finished work back to the clerk — the tab grows a Hand in button once it is done — and she pays in guild standing. At the very top of the ladder there is nowhere further for your name to go, and she says so rather than pretending otherwise.

The guild also keeps a quartermaster, and the clerk mentions them at the same time as the board. What they sell is components — the parts equipment is assembled from — and specifically the ones no other shop in the game stocks at all, which is most of them.
Which shelf they open depends on your rank: the plainest parts from the bottom, and each step up the ladder unlocks a tier of better ones. The last tier waits until the very top, because a part from it skips a long stretch of crafting.
You pay in money, not standing — spending standing would cost you the rank that let you buy. But standing does set the price: the guild is its own market region, so every rank you climb makes the counter cheaper.

A fetch says where the thing is gathered, so a notice for thirty of something is never a notice you have no way to fill. If you want out anyway, Give up hands the notice back and costs guild standing — about half what the job would have paid, with the price written on the button, and never more standing than you have. The notice is gone rather than pinned back up.

  • Money:
    • 10 W = 1 C, 100 C = 1 S, 100 S = 1 G W: Wood, C: Copper, S: Silver, G: Gold
  • Prices
    • Traders sell items for prices a lot higher than what they would pay for same item (they need to make a living, after all).
      "Haggling" skill slightly reduces price difference, at max level the value above 100% is cut in half (meaning 200% will turn into 150%, 300% will turn into 200%, etc)
      Reputation also reduces prices for traders in the area. View your current reputation in the "Data" tab.
      Standing is not only a discount. Settlements spend it: six actions across the slums and the town square answer to it, and some doors open on it rather than on a skill. You will only see a region listed once you have earned something there.
      Four of the five are places: the Village, the Slums, the Town and the swampland tribe, which you raise by bringing its workshops what they asked for. The fifth is the Guild, which is not a place at all — you earn it by doing the guild's kind of work rather than by being seen in a district. Two houses pay into it: the merchant guild's factor, and the clerk at the Adventurer's guild in town, whose board is described under Guild work below.
  • Market saturation
    • As you sell more of enemy loot, its price will slowly go down, stopping at 1/10th of initial value. It applies to all traders.
      The price will recover over time, but it is a very slow process.

      Saturation values are separate for different regions. They trickle between them every in-game day, until reaching balance.
  • Crafting subcategories:
    • Items: mostly mundane things, such as consumables and materials for further crafting, but also includes cool trinkets to wear
      If the materials you put in carry a quality of their own, the result gets one too, rolled around theirs - a good fish makes a good meal and a poor one does not. Most materials have no quality, and those recipes are unchanged; fishing is the one activity outside the workshop that gives what it gathers a quality
    • Components: things that can be assembled into equipment of all kinds.
      Their quality doesn't directly translate to equipment stats and so their own statistics are displayed as "base value", as in value that they would contribute to a 100% quality piece of equipment
    • Equipment: the actual stuff that you can put on. Mostly created from components, with the exception of 'clothing' which functions more like wearable components
      Quality is based on skill level, quality of components (for non-clothing equipment) and either tier of components (for non-clothing) or tier of resulting clothing item
  • What you have and have not made. A recipe you have never once made carries a small ○ after its name. It is not a lock — you may well be able to make it right now — it only marks the difference between "I can make this" and "I have made this", which nothing else on the page could tell you.
    Only what I can make hides every recipe you do not currently have the materials and skill for, which is the shorter of the two lists by a long way once the categories fill up.
  • You can obtain resources from passive activities in locations and/or as a loot from combat; items that can be used for crafting will often (but not always) mention it in their descriptions
    For passive gathering activities, things such as time needed, success chance, and number of gained items, are all affected by skill level.
  • XP and quality caps:
    • Quality of equipment is capped at 100% + 2.8% per skill level.
      Quality of components (excluding clothing, which falls under equipment) is capped at 100% + 2% per skill level.
      Items made out of materials that carry a quality use the same cap as components.
      It exists mainly to make higher tier materials catch up with lower tier ones at some point.
    • XP gains from item recipes start going down once you are a few levels over the value needed to max out the success chance, from there they steadily go down to 1. XP gains from component/equipment recipes start going down once relevant skill is more than 8*item_tier, this value is however also multiplied by sqrt of item's rarity multiplier (e.g. sqrt(1.1) for 'uncommon'). They go down to 0.5*material_count for components and down to 1 for equipments.
  • AP / EP:
    • Summary of stats and skills that affect the chance to hit/evade. It doesn't include situational modifiers (e.g. from skills based on the type of an enemy), but does include environmental modifiers (e.g. lower EP in "narrow" areas)
    • Scaling is non-linear.
    • AP equal to 5 times the enemy EP, results in ~98% chance to hit the target.
    • AP equal to twice the enemy EP, results in ~80% chance to hit the target.
    • AP equal to enemy EP, results in a bit over 50% chance to hit the target.
    • AP equal to half of the enemy EP, results in ~24% chance to hit the target.
    • AP equal to 1/5 of the enemy EP, results in ~5% chance to hit the target.

  • Hit / dodge chance
    • Displayed in an enemy during combat
    • They represent ENEMY's chance to hit or to dodge attacks

  • Defensive measures
    • "Dodge", if triggered, will always completely negate damage.
    • Shield can absorb damage and has very high base chance to trigger, but usually won't be strong enough to reduce it to 0, although it is possible.
    • "Defense" provides a flat reduction to damage taken. It is applied after shield dmg reduction.
      It can't reduce damage taken to less than 1 or 10% of original value, whichever is larger. However, if less than that gets through the shield, that value will be used instead, allowing for very small dmg values and/or full negation.

  • Attack speed:
    • Directly translates to how quickly someone attacks in comparison to another fight participant. 2x more => 2x faster. 2x less => 2x slower.
    • To avoid insanity and potential problems, when new fight begins, both character and enemies have their attack cooldowns scaled up so that no one attacks faster than once per second.

  • Stamina:
    • When stamina drops to 0, character's attack speed and attack power are cut in half.
    • Penalty can be lowered via the "Persistence" skill, leveled by fighting with no stamina
    • It can also be delayed by raising the max stamina and/or using food items, which provide stamina regeneration in combat

  • Combat stances:
    • Provide different buffs and penalties, being helpful in different situations, in exchange for higher stamina costs.
    • Some enemies react to how you are standing, not just to your numbers. A swarm that you meet with a point closes in on you and one you meet with a broad stance goes backwards; a frog's toxins care how much of you is in front of them; a stinger finds a body that has committed to a swing. Reading the enemy is part of picking the stance.
    • Switching stance mid-combat will reset your attack bar (no cheesing allowed).

  • Loot and drop rates:
    • Every item an enemy can drop has its own chance, and most of them are small - a turtle shell or a giant crab claw is half a percent.
    • Some skills multiply those chances for a whole kind of creature rather than for one enemy: "Butchering" for beasts, "Shellwork" for the things that come out of water. They stack where a creature is both.
    • Fighting several enemies at once lowers what each one drops. A group is faster than the same enemies one at a time, and that is what it costs.
    • An enemy's bestiary entry shows its drops with your current skills already applied, so it is the place to check whether a skill is doing anything.
  • Certain activities are limited by the time of the day or by the season.
  • Some places and some people are seasonal too. One region changes twice a year on its own, without telling you: the shelf it sells from is different, the things you overhear there are different, and somewhere inland a clerk has something to say about it. Nothing announces any of that. If a place feels different from how you remember it, look at the date.
  • During night (20 to 4) in normal areas, and whole time in dark areas (e.g. caves), character receives additional penalties to hit/evasion chance.
    This penalty can be reduced with "Night vision" skill, which gains xp passively just for being in such place.

  • Some combat areas have special properties, which result in penalties to certain stats. E.g. some areas might not offer enough movement space to properly dodge or make a good swing with weapon
    They usually have a corresponding skill that lowers the penalty and is usually leveled by simply being in such place
  • Staying for too long in a low temperature will trigger certain negative effects, remember to bring warm clothes. Being wet will reduce your tolerance to cold.
    Temperatures differ between zones - for some they change less, for others they are static. The system is however fully deterministic.
  • The seasons are indeed supposed to start on different dates than they do in our world, don't worry about that~
  • XP:
    • Harder enemies generally provide more xp. It is not affected by area modifiers, but is affected by how many enemies are in a group (raised to 0.334, giving x2 in a fight against 8)
    • There is also a lot of bonuses from multiple sources (mainly skill milestones and completed books, but also a nice multiplier based on hero level).

  • Unlocks:
    • New locations, actions, and activities, can be unlocked by talking with someone, by reaching certain levels in skills, by clearing enemy zones (sometimes it might need to be beaten more than once), and by succeeding in actions
  • They multiply leveling speed of their children if these are of lower level
  • They stay on the same level as their highest-leveled child
  • Sorting the inventory. Four buttons, labelled short so they fit: Name, Type, Value and Newest. Clicking the one already chosen reverses it. Newest is by when the item reached you rather than by anything about the item, which is the one you want after a long fight or a long haul of gathering. Hovering any of them says what it does. Whichever you pick is remembered, direction included, so the list opens the way you left it next time — the storage keeps its own choice separately.
  • Equipment quality
    • All equippable items come with "quality" parameter. It directly multiplies item's basic bonus (attack for weapons, defense for armors, max block for shields) and value.
      On top of that, higher quality ranks (starting at uncommon, which begins at 100%) provide another multiplier (based solely on rank) to not only basic bonus, but also to all other stats it has. That bonus applies at a reduced rate to some stats that would be otherwise too powerful.

        50-99%      common         white      x1

        200-246%    legendary      orange     x2

    • As of version v0.6.7, the highest item quality any shop offers is 120% - with one exception, and it does not keep shop hours
